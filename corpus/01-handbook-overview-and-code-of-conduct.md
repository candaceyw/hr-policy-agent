# Handbook Overview and Code of Conduct

## Purpose
This handbook provides the general framework for how Northwind Robotics, Inc. expects employees to work, communicate, and comply with company policies. It summarizes the expectations that apply to workplace behavior, time away from work, remote arrangements, benefits eligibility, expense handling, security, and problem reporting. The handbook is a practical guide to our policies and procedures and should be read together with the more specific policy documents referenced throughout and with applicable local law.

This handbook does not create a contractual promise of continued employment, guaranteed benefits, or specific job duties beyond those described in a written offer letter, employment agreement, or applicable local law. Employment with Northwind is at-will where permitted by law. Northwind may update or revise policies from time to time to reflect business needs, legal requirements, and operational changes; material changes are communicated through the internal portal and by email, and the current version of each policy always supersedes earlier versions.

## About Northwind Robotics
Northwind Robotics designs robotics hardware and the software that runs it. The company employs roughly 650 people. The US headquarters is in Austin, Texas, with additional US offices in Seattle, New York, San Francisco, Denver, Chicago, and Miami. International staff in Ireland are employed through Northwind Robotics Ireland (Dublin). People Operations owns and maintains HR policy; the Director of People Operations is the final internal escalation point for policy questions and disputes.

## Company Values
Northwind Robotics is guided by values of integrity, accountability, respect, and customer focus. We expect employees to:

- act with honesty and professionalism,
- treat colleagues, customers, and partners with respect,
- communicate clearly and responsibly,
- protect confidential information,
- raise concerns early rather than allowing risk to grow,
- support a workplace that is safe, inclusive, and productive.

## Scope of the Handbook
This handbook applies to employees, temporary staff, interns, and contractors where their work or role falls within company policy and the terms of their engagement. Some benefits and entitlements described here — for example paid time off, company-paid leave, and the benefits package — apply only to regular employees and, in several cases, only to full-time employees working 30 or more hours per week. Each policy states its own eligibility rules.

Where a policy conflicts with local law, a collective bargaining obligation, or a specific written employment agreement, the governing legal requirement or agreement controls, and the rest of the policy still applies. Managers and HR may layer role-specific requirements, department procedures, or local legal requirements on top of this baseline, but may not waive a company minimum without People Operations approval.

## Standard Working Hours
The standard workweek is 40 hours. Standard business hours are 09:00 to 17:00 local time. Core collaboration hours, when employees are expected to be reachable for meetings and time-sensitive work, are 10:00 to 15:00 local time. Non-exempt employees are paid overtime at 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a week, and overtime must be approved by the manager in advance. Details are in the Compensation and Payroll Policy.

## Workplace Expectations
Employees are expected to follow company policies and to behave consistently with the values above. Certain standards apply to everyone:

- attendance and punctuality in accordance with the employee's schedule and manager direction,
- use of company time, equipment, and resources for legitimate business purposes,
- protection of confidential or regulated data in line with the Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy and the Information Classification Standard,
- respect for colleagues, customers, and partners,
- truthful reporting of time, expenses, and workplace issues,
- adherence to safety, security, and compliance procedures.

A policy is not optional simply because it is not repeated in every tool or workflow. When a question arises, employees should ask their manager, People Operations, or the designated support contact before acting.

## Code of Conduct
Northwind prohibits discrimination, harassment, retaliation, abusive conduct, and other behavior that undermines an inclusive and respectful workplace. Employees must not discriminate against others because of race, color, religion, sex, pregnancy, gender identity, sexual orientation, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law. Harassment includes unwelcome conduct that creates a hostile or intimidating environment, unreasonably interferes with work performance, or affects equal employment opportunity.

Examples of prohibited conduct include bullying, threatening remarks, demeaning comments, sexual harassment, unwanted physical contact, intimidation, retaliation against people who report concerns or participate in investigations, and misuse of authority to target an employee or applicant unfairly.

## Respectful Workplace Standards
Employees are expected to communicate professionally and to handle conflict productively. This includes:

- speaking respectfully in meetings and one-on-one discussions,
- avoiding hostility, intimidation, or public humiliation,
- using appropriate judgment in email, chat, and other digital communication,
- raising concerns through proper channels when a problem is not resolved informally.

Managers are expected to model respectful conduct and to respond promptly and fairly to reports of misconduct or harassment. Ignoring a credible report is itself a policy violation.

## Reporting Concerns
Employees who experience or observe misconduct should report it promptly. Reports may be made to a manager, to People Operations, through the confidential ethics and concerns channel, or through the formal grievance process described in the Workplace Conduct and Grievance Procedure. Reports are acknowledged within two business days, and investigations target completion within 30 days. Reports may be made in confidence to the extent permitted by law and the investigation process. Retaliation against a person who reports a concern in good faith or cooperates with a review is strictly prohibited.

## Investigations and Fair Process
Northwind reviews concerns objectively. Investigations may involve interviews, review of records and communications, and consideration of relevant policies and local law. Employees involved in an investigation are expected to cooperate honestly and to maintain confidentiality to the extent the process requires. Where the facts support it, the company may take corrective action ranging from coaching to written warnings, removal of system access, reassignment, or termination of employment.

## Security and Information Use
Employees must protect company information and use company systems only for legitimate business needs. This includes respecting access controls, safeguarding credentials, enabling multi-factor authentication, and following the Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy and the Information Classification Standard when working in the office, remotely, or on travel. If an employee is unsure whether a particular use, tool, or storage location is allowed, they should ask IT or the security team before proceeding.

## Time Away, Leave, and Attendance
Time away from work is governed by the PTO and Vacation Policy, the Holidays and Company Closure Policy, the Leave of Absence Policy, and the Parental and Family Leave Policy. Time off is not approved until it is confirmed through the appropriate process. Requests are evaluated against staffing needs, manager approval, notice requirements, and policy eligibility.

## Expense, Travel, and Benefits
Employees are expected to use company resources responsibly and to request reimbursement only for approved business expenses that are properly documented, as described in the Expense and Reimbursement Policy and the Travel Policy. Benefits eligibility depends on employment type, scheduled hours, and tenure, as described in the Benefits Guide.

## Remote and Flexible Work
Remote and hybrid work are available in roles where the work can be performed effectively outside an office and where security, availability, and legal requirements can be met. Remote arrangements require manager approval, and fully remote arrangements require Director-level approval. Working from a different US state for more than 30 days, or from another country, requires additional review under the Out-of-State and International Remote Work Policy.

## Employee, Manager, and HR Responsibilities
- Employees: know the policies that apply to their situation, ask questions early, report concerns, and keep their information current.
- Managers: apply policy consistently, document decisions, escalate issues that affect pay, leave rights, or legal compliance, and never retaliate.
- People Operations: maintain the policies, advise managers and employees, run investigations, and ensure compliance with local law.

## Conclusion
This handbook is the framework that helps employees understand the company's standards and the rules that govern daily work. It does not replace specific procedures, local legal obligations, or manager guidance. When a policy is unclear, employees should ask for clarification before acting. Northwind would rather answer a question early than manage a problem later.
