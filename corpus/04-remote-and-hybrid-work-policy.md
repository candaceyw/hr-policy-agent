# Remote and Hybrid Work Policy

## Purpose
This policy describes when Remote and Hybrid Work arrangements are available, how approval works, and what expectations apply when employees work outside a company office. Northwind supports remote work where it is operationally effective, legally compliant, and consistent with business needs.

## Eligibility
Employees may be eligible for remote or hybrid work when their role allows work to be performed effectively outside the office and when they meet the company requirements for location, performance, and security. Examples of positions often eligible for remote or hybrid work include software, research, design, operations support, marketing, and planning roles that do not require constant onsite presence.

Positions involving physical equipment handling, customer-facing support, lab work, manufacturing operations, or in-person collaboration may not be suitable for remote arrangements. Managers should review role requirements before approving a remote schedule.

## Core Eligibility Standards
Employees generally must meet the following criteria before remote work is approved:

- performance is satisfactory or there is no significant corrective action in progress,
- the employee can maintain a reliable workspace and internet connection,
- the employee can maintain required working hours and responsiveness,
- the employee follows security and confidentiality rules,
- the employee is in a state or country where the work arrangement is legally and operationally supportable.

## Approval Process
Remote work arrangements require manager approval and may require HR or business-owner review depending on the employee's role, location, or tenure. Employees should not assume that remote work is automatically approved or permanently approved.

When an arrangement is approved, it should be documented in writing and include:

- the remote-work location,
- expected schedule or work pattern,
- business hours and availability expectations,
- any required security or equipment provisions,
- review dates or re-evaluation points.

## Schedule Expectations
Remote and hybrid employees are expected to maintain agreed-upon working hours and be reachable during those hours. Managers may define core collaboration hours for meetings, support events, or team check-ins. If a manager needs a hybrid employee to be in the office on a recurring basis, that requirement should be clearly communicated.

Employees must also maintain appropriate communication habits, including responding to messages, attending meetings, and staying aligned with project deadlines.

## Work Location Requirements
Employees working remotely must maintain a secure and distraction-free workspace and a stable internet connection. Work must be performed from an approved location, and employees should notify the company of material changes to that location.

Employees must not work from locations that create legal, tax, security, or compliance risk without prior approval. Any change in location may require review under the out-of-state or international remote-work policy.

## Equipment and Security
Employees working remotely must use company-approved devices and systems, and they must comply with the Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy. Company data may not be stored on unapproved storage, external systems, or personal cloud subscriptions unless authorized by IT or security.

Employees are responsible for ensuring their home workspace is safe, secure, and suitable for work. The company may provide additional equipment or ergonomic support where it is reasonably needed and approved.

## Business Continuity and Team Coverage
Remote work arrangements may be adjusted or suspended if staffing needs, business continuity concerns, or team dynamics require it. Managers may require more onsite presence in projects where frequent collaboration or operational coverage is required.

Managers should document any concerns and communicate them clearly, and HR should be involved if the issue could affect an employee's performance, attendance, or legal compliance.

## Non-Compliance and Review
Remote work may be discontinued or modified if the employee fails to meet performance expectations, fails to maintain a compliant work setup, violates company policy, or fails to provide appropriate notice of changes in location or schedule.

Managers should not remove remote access arbitrarily; they should document the concern, review whether the issue is isolated or recurring, and consider whether a remediation or temporary adjustment would be more appropriate.

## Summary
Remote work is a benefit available in roles and situations where it supports business needs and meets company standards. Approval is not automatic, and remote work is conditioned on security, availability, and compliance obligations.
