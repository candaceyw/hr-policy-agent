Workplace Conduct and Grievance Procedure

Purpose

This policy states the expectation that everyone at Northwind Robotics works in a respectful, lawful, and professional environment, and explains how concerns about workplace conduct are raised, investigated, and resolved. It supports a culture grounded in respect, accountability, and fair process, and it works with the Code of Conduct in the Handbook Overview.

Expected Standards of Conduct

Employees treat coworkers, clients, vendors, customers, and managers with respect and professionalism and comply with anti-discrimination, anti-harassment, and anti-retaliation standards and applicable law. Northwind does not tolerate:

  • discriminatory comments, decisions, or conduct based on a protected characteristic,
  • harassment, including sexual harassment, and bullying,
  • threats, intimidation, stalking, or violence,
  • retaliation against a person who raises a concern or takes part in a review,
  • abusive behavior in the workplace or in work communications and online spaces,
  • interfering with another person's ability to work safely and respectfully,
  • conduct that creates a hostile work environment.

Employees must also avoid conduct that undermines the company's values, safety, or business integrity, whether at work, at a work event, or while representing the company.

Manager Responsibilities

Managers model respectful leadership and act on concerns. A manager who receives a report or personally observes possible discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or abusive conduct must escalate it to People Operations promptly and must not attempt to resolve a serious matter informally or on their own. Managers must not retaliate and must keep the matter confidential. When a concern involves the manager, the employee may go directly to People Operations, the confidential ethics channel, or a skip-level leader.

How to Raise a Concern

An employee who experiences or witnesses misconduct, or reasonably suspects a serious policy violation, should raise it promptly through any of:

  • their manager,
  • People Operations,
  • the confidential ethics and concerns channel (phone or web, anonymous where local law allows),
  • a designated grievance contact for their region.

Provide as much detail as possible — what happened, when and where, who was involved, who else saw it, and any records. A report does not need to be perfect or certain to be taken seriously. Good-faith reports are protected even if the review does not substantiate the concern.

Grievance Procedure and Timeline

1. Acknowledgment — People Operations acknowledges the report to the reporter within 2 business days and explains next steps.

2. Assessment — People Operations decides whether interim measures are needed (for example, adjusting reporting lines or schedules, or a leave) to protect the people involved during the review. Interim measures are not a finding of fault.

3. Investigation — a fair, objective review that may include interviews with the reporter, the respondent, and witnesses, and review of documents and communications. The investigation targets completion within 30 days; complex matters may take longer, and People Operations keeps the parties informed of status.

4. Findings — the investigator determines whether a policy was more likely than not violated and documents the basis.

5. Outcome — the company decides on any corrective action and, where appropriate, tells the reporter that the matter was addressed. Specific disciplinary details about another employee are kept confidential.

Corrective Action

Depending on the findings, corrective action may include coaching, mandatory training, a written warning, a change in duties or reporting line, removal of system or facility access, a final warning, or termination of employment. The company may also make structural changes — additional training, supervision changes, or policy updates — to reduce future risk.

Non-Retaliation

Retaliation against someone for raising a concern in good faith, refusing to take part in prohibited conduct, or cooperating with a review is a serious violation and is itself grounds for corrective action. Retaliation can include termination, demotion, exclusion, unwarranted negative reviews, schedule or assignment changes made to punish, threats, or hostility. An employee who believes they are experiencing retaliation should report it the same way as any other concern.

Privacy and Confidentiality

Reports and investigation records are shared only with people who need them to review, decide, or act, and are stored with restricted access under the Information Classification Standard. Participants are asked to keep the matter confidential to protect the process and the people involved. Employees should not conduct their own parallel investigation or make public accusations before the review is complete, as that can compromise fairness and create risk for everyone.

Appeals

A reporter or a respondent who believes the process was not followed or the outcome was unreasonable may appeal in writing to the Director of People Operations within 10 business days of being informed of the outcome. The appeal review considers whether the process was fair and the conclusion supported; it is not a full re-investigation unless significant new information is presented.

Escalation

Matters involving repeated misconduct, serious allegations (violence, threats, significant discrimination or harassment), a senior leader, or a potential legal violation are escalated to People Operations leadership and Legal, and may involve an external investigator. No employee should feel discouraged from escalating a serious concern they believe is not being handled appropriately.

Responsibilities

  • Employees: raise concerns promptly and in good faith, cooperate with reviews, and maintain confidentiality.
  • Managers: escalate serious concerns to People Operations, protect reporters, and never retaliate.
  • People Operations and Legal: run a fair and timely process, decide interim measures and outcomes, and handle appeals and escalations.

Summary

Northwind requires a respectful, lawful workplace and prohibits harassment, discrimination, and retaliation. Concerns can be raised with a manager, People Operations, or the confidential ethics channel. Reports are acknowledged within 2 business days, investigations target 30 days, and outcomes range from coaching to termination. Good-faith reporters are protected from retaliation, and either party may appeal to the Director of People Operations within 10 business days.
