# Performance and Development Policy

## Purpose
This policy describes how Northwind Robotics sets expectations, reviews performance, supports development, and addresses performance that falls below expectations. The aim is clear goals, regular feedback, fair evaluation, and real opportunities to grow.

## Goal Setting
At the start of each year, and when a role changes, every employee and manager agree on written goals for the period. Goals describe outcomes, not just activity, and connect to team and company priorities. Managers revisit goals during the year if priorities shift and record any change so the employee is evaluated against current expectations.

## Ongoing Feedback and One-on-Ones
Managers hold a regular one-on-one with each direct report — normally weekly or biweekly — for coaching, blockers, and feedback. Feedback should be timely and specific. Nothing in the formal review cycle should be a surprise if one-on-ones are happening.

## Review Cycle
There are two formal touchpoints each year:

- **Mid-year check-in (June)** — a written progress review against goals, with development discussion. It does not produce a numeric rating.
- **Annual review (December)** — a written evaluation of the full year against goals and role expectations, resulting in an overall **rating on a 1–5 scale** (1 = does not meet expectations, 3 = meets expectations, 5 = outstanding). Employees complete a self-assessment first, and managers may gather peer input.

Annual ratings inform the merit review, whose increases take effect **April 1** (see the Compensation and Payroll Policy). Ratings are calibrated across teams for consistency before they are finalized.

## New Hires
New employees have a structured first 90 days with 30-, 60-, and 90-day check-ins. If a new hire is not on track by 90 days, the manager and People Operations decide whether to extend support, start a formal improvement plan, or end the employment.

## Professional Development Benefit
Regular employees are eligible for **up to $2,000 per calendar year** in reimbursement for job-relevant development — courses, certifications, exam fees, books, and eligible tuition — with **advance manager approval**. The amount is **prorated for part-time** employees based on scheduled hours. Reimbursement requires proof of completion (or proof of enrollment for multi-term programs) and is submitted through the expense system. Unused amounts do not carry over. If the company also pays for the time (for example, work hours spent in class), that is agreed in advance.

The company pays for **one domestic conference per employee per year** with manager approval; travel follows the Travel Policy. Internal training and manager-nominated programs are funded separately and do not count against the $2,000.

## Internal Mobility
Employees who have been in their current role for at least 12 months (or have manager support to apply earlier) may apply for internal openings. Internal candidates are told the outcome and given feedback. A transfer's start date is coordinated so the current team has a reasonable handover, normally within 4 weeks of the offer.

## Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)
When performance is below expectations and coaching has not resolved it, the manager, with People Operations, may place the employee on a **Performance Improvement Plan** lasting **at least 30 to 60 days**. A PIP states the specific gaps, the measurable expectations, the support the company will provide, the review points, and the consequence of not meeting the plan (which may include termination). An employee who meets the plan returns to normal status; sustained problems after a PIP may lead to termination. Serious misconduct is handled under the Workplace Conduct and Grievance Procedure, not the PIP process.

## Recognition
Managers and the company recognize strong work through the recognition program, spot awards where a plan exists, and consideration in promotion and merit decisions. Recognition is not a substitute for a market-appropriate salary, which is managed through the pay-band and merit process.

## Documentation and Fair Process
Reviews, PIPs, and significant coaching conversations are documented. Employees may add written comments to their review. Performance records are confidential and stored with restricted access under the Information Classification Standard. Evaluations must be based on job-related performance and never on a protected characteristic or on retaliation.

## Responsibilities
- Employees: agree on goals, seek and act on feedback, complete self-assessments, and plan development with their manager.
- Managers: set clear goals, give regular feedback, evaluate fairly and consistently, and use a documented PIP rather than a sudden termination for performance issues.
- People Operations: run the review and calibration cycle, advise on PIPs, and ensure evaluations are fair and lawful.

## Summary
Employees set written goals yearly, meet regularly with their manager, have a mid-year check-in in June and a rated annual review (1–5 scale) in December, and ratings feed the April 1 merit cycle. The development benefit reimburses up to $2,000 a year of job-relevant learning with advance approval (prorated for part-time), plus one domestic conference a year. Underperformance is addressed through a documented 30–60 day improvement plan before termination.
