# Workplace Health and Safety Policy

## Purpose
This policy describes how Northwind Robotics keeps workplaces — offices, labs, and home workspaces — safe, how employees report injuries and hazards, and how the company responds to emergencies. It applies to every employee, contractor, and intern, wherever they perform company work.

## Shared Responsibilities
Northwind provides safe facilities, equipment, training, and procedures and complies with applicable occupational health and safety law. Employees follow safety procedures, use equipment as intended, keep their work area orderly, and speak up about hazards. No employee is ever expected to do work they reasonably believe is unsafe; raising a safety concern in good faith is protected and never a basis for retaliation.

## Reporting Injuries and Illnesses
Report any work-related injury or illness — however minor — to your manager and People Operations **within 24 hours**. For anything beyond minor first aid, seek medical care first, then report. People Operations initiates the workers' compensation process. **Workers' compensation covers a work-related injury regardless of where the work is performed**, including an approved home workspace or a business trip; the applicable program may vary by location. Prompt reporting protects the employee's ability to claim and helps the company fix the cause.

## Hazard Reporting
Report unsafe conditions — a spill, a damaged cable, a blocked exit, a broken chair, faulty lab equipment, a security or lighting problem in a parking area — to Facilities or your manager as soon as you notice them. Urgent hazards that could cause immediate harm should be reported immediately and the area made safe or avoided until it is resolved. The company tracks hazard reports and corrective actions and does not penalize good-faith reports.

## Office and Lab Safety
- Know the locations of exits, fire extinguishers, first-aid kits, and automated external defibrillators (AEDs) at your site.
- Keep walkways and exits clear; do not prop fire doors.
- Lab and hardware areas have additional rules: required personal protective equipment, safe handling of tools, batteries, and materials, machine-guarding, and lockout procedures. Only trained, authorized employees operate lab and shop equipment.
- Visitors sign in, are escorted in non-public areas, and follow site safety rules.

## Ergonomics
Employees may request an **ergonomic assessment** of their office or home workspace at any time; People Operations arranges it. Recommended standard adjustments (monitor height, chair, keyboard tray) are supported, and remote employees may use the home-office setup stipend in the Onboarding and Equipment Provisioning Policy toward ergonomic equipment.

## Remote Workspace Safety
Employees with an approved remote or hybrid arrangement complete a brief **remote workspace self-assessment at least once a year** and after a location change: a stable work surface and chair, adequate lighting, no trip or fire hazards around cabling, a working smoke detector in the area, and a way to work without strain. Report a home-workspace injury the same way as any other work injury, within 24 hours.

## Emergency Procedures
Each site has an emergency plan covering evacuation, shelter-in-place, severe weather, medical emergencies, and utility failures. Follow instructions from site wardens and emergency services. In a fire alarm, leave by the nearest safe exit and go to the assembly point; do not use elevators. After any evacuation, check in with your manager or the warden so everyone is accounted for. Company closures and weather events are handled under the Holidays and Company Closure Policy.

## Workplace Violence Prevention
Threats, intimidation, weapons on company premises (except as law expressly permits), and violence are prohibited and are treated as serious misconduct under the Workplace Conduct and Grievance Procedure. Report a threat or an act of violence immediately to security or local emergency services and to People Operations. The company will take protective measures — access changes, security support, coordination with law enforcement, and referrals — as needed.

## Substance Use
Employees must not be impaired by alcohol or drugs while working or on company premises in a way that affects safety or performance. Legally prescribed medication is permitted; if it could affect safety-sensitive work, the employee should speak with People Operations about adjustments. Company-sanctioned events that serve alcohol still require safe, professional conduct and safe transportation.

## Health Screenings and Accommodations
The company supports reasonable accommodations for disability and pregnancy-related needs through an interactive process with People Operations. Wellness resources, including the Employee Assistance Program, are described in the Benefits Guide.

## Responsibilities
- Employees: follow safety procedures, report injuries within 24 hours and hazards promptly, complete the annual remote self-assessment, and never perform work they reasonably believe is unsafe.
- Managers: ensure their team is trained, act on reported hazards, and support injured employees through the process.
- Facilities, Security, and People Operations: maintain safe sites and emergency plans, investigate incidents, run workers' compensation, and track corrective actions.

## Summary
Everyone shares responsibility for safety. Report work-related injuries to your manager and People Operations within 24 hours; workers' compensation applies wherever you work, including at home. Report hazards as soon as you see them. Know your site's exits, extinguishers, and AEDs. Remote employees do a yearly workspace self-check and can request an ergonomic assessment. Threats and violence are treated as serious misconduct, and no one is expected to do work they reasonably believe is unsafe.
