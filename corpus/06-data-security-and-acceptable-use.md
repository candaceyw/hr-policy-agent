# Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy

## Purpose
This policy sets the minimum expectations for handling Northwind Robotics' information, systems, and devices. It protects company data, customer information, employee information, and internal business records from unauthorized access, loss, or misuse.

## General Principles
Employees must use company systems and data only for legitimate work purposes. Company resources may not be used for personal activities that create risk, interfere with business operations, or violate confidentiality expectations.

Employees are expected to:

- protect credentials and use approved authentication methods,
- keep devices updated and configured according to policy,
- use only approved software, communication channels, and storage systems,
- treat confidential information as sensitive even when it is not marked as such,
- report suspicious activity or security concerns immediately.

## Confidentiality and Access Control
Confidential information includes business plans, customer data, internal policies, employee records, financial information, and any information with a confidentiality designation or reasonable expectation of privacy. Employees should access only the information needed to do their jobs and should not share it unnecessarily.

Employees must not access, share, or download data for personal interest, curiosity, or convenience. Access rights are granted for business need and may be revoked if misuse occurs.

## Passwords and Authentication
Employees must use strong, unique credentials and must not share their passwords or authentication codes. Multi-factor authentication must be enabled for company systems where required. Employees should not write down passwords in insecure locations or reuse them across personal accounts.

If a device, token, or account is lost or suspected to be compromised, the employee must report the issue immediately to IT or security support.

## Device and Network Use
Company devices should be used only for approved business activity and should be protected from theft, tampering, and unsafe network use. Employees should avoid using public Wi-Fi for sensitive company work unless company-approved security tools are active.

Employees must not install software, browser extensions, or remote-access utilities that are not approved by IT or security. Any unauthorized installation may be treated as a security violation.

## Remote Work and Security Expectations
Employees working remotely must follow the same security expectations as onsite employees. This includes using approved devices, avoiding unauthorized cloud storage, restricting access to sensitive information, and handling confidential content only in approved environments.

Employees should secure printers, home networks, and personal devices where they are used for work. If a remote worker is unsure whether a device or service is compliant, they should ask IT before using it.

## Acceptable Use Standards
The following are examples of unacceptable use:

- using company systems to view, create, or share unauthorized content,
- storing company data in unapproved personal tools or unauthorized file-sharing platforms,
- bypassing controls or attempting to circumvent security policies,
- using company systems for harassment, discrimination, or unlawful activity,
- installing or using unapproved software on company-managed devices.

Employees may access company systems for business-related learning, research, and work tasks, but not for unrelated or excessive personal activity.

## Data Handling and Storage
Employees should keep data in approved company-managed repositories and use the correct classification and retention practices. If data must be shared externally, the employee should use the approved company process and ensure correct permissions and approvals are in place.

Data should not be downloaded or copied onto local personal storage unless specifically approved and necessary for business purposes.

## Reporting Security Issues
Any suspected compromise, data leak, lost device, phishing event, or unusual system access must be reported immediately to the IT/security team and the employee's manager. Delayed reporting can increase harm and may create additional corrective obligations.

Employees should not attempt to investigate a suspected breach in a way that could destroy evidence or patch a vulnerability without authorizing security personnel.

## Consequences
Failure to comply with this policy may result in corrective action, removal of access, restricted system usage, or disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment, depending on the seriousness and circumstances of the violation.
